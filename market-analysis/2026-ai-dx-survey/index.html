Executive summary

48 developers responded. Read Results of the 2026 Wagtail DX with AI survey for the key findings. Headline signals across the dataset:

  • AI use is mainstream in the community. 52% use AI tools daily or always when working on Wagtail (Daily 31%, Always 21%), 71% at least weekly, and only 10% never.
  • Most respondents are already past the beginner stage. 85% rate themselves Familiar, Confident, or Advanced with AI-assisted development.
  • Current Wagtail experience with AI is workable but not great. 77% say tooling works "somewhat well" or "very well" on Wagtail projects; 10% say "poorly", 13% haven't tried.
  • Most-used tool types: Chat interfaces (69%), agentic coding CLIs (54%), IDE autocomplete (44%), agentic coding in IDE (40%).
  • Top engineering tasks where AI is used systematically (avg 0–3): Code generation 2.06, Debugging 1.94, Test generation 1.92, Refactoring 1.89. Lowest: Accessibility checking 0.93.
  • Top Wagtail-specific tasks: Debugging Wagtail issues 1.70, Generating page types/models 1.60, Front-end development 1.60. Lowest: Wagtail API integrations 1.25.
  • Most-wanted Wagtail improvements for AI-assisted dev: Better Django capabilities docs on Wagtail (2.05), Official Wagtail rules/skills (2.02), Type hints (1.93). Lowest: Project-integrated MCP for automation (1.37).
  • Most-wanted CMS-side AI features: Image alt-text generation 2.34, Natural language search 2.10, Structured metadata generation 2.02. Lowest: First-draft writing assistance & content personalisation (1.31 each).
  • Top adoption considerations (importance, avg 0–3): Ethics and bias 2.20, Legal liability 2.13, Cost 2.11, IP/licensing 2.00. Environmental impact also rates 1.87.
  • Existing Wagtail AI efforts have low adoption: llms.txt for docs 21%, Wagtail AI 15%, Upgrades agent skill 13% — likely an awareness gap as much as a fit gap.

Methodology

Rationale

The survey was run to better understand the Wagtail community's expectations around AI so the project can plan appropriate Developer Experience (DX) improvements. A wide spectrum of attitudes towards AI was expected — enthusiasts, sceptics, and everyone in between — and the survey was designed to capture that variety. Three explicit goals:

  • Establish current levels of AI adoption among Wagtail developers.
  • Establish awareness of what the project has already done in this space (Wagtail AI, llms.txt for Wagtail docs, Upgrades agent skill).
  • Float concrete improvements the team is interested in and gauge their helpfulness.

Direction-setting will follow the Wagtail AI guiding principles.

Sample

  • n = 48 respondents. Self-selected via the public announcement; all questions optional.
  • Per-question response counts can be lower than 48 because optional questions or matrix rows could be skipped — percentages on this page are computed against the respondents who answered that specific question, not against the full 48.
  • The Existing Wagtail AI efforts chart uses a hard-coded denominator of 48 in the SQL, so its percentages are out of all respondents rather than out of those who answered the question.

Question structure

The survey had two main sections:

  • Your current usage — how often AI is used, self-rated experience, kinds of tools, how well current AI tooling works on Wagtail, systematic use across engineering tasks (matrix), Wagtail-specific tasks (matrix), and use of existing Wagtail AI efforts.
  • Our direction — importance of AI-adoption considerations (matrix), helpfulness of possible improvements to Wagtail and its ecosystem (matrix), and helpfulness of CMS-side features for editors working with AI (matrix).

Two free-text questions are included in the underlying CSVs but not visualised on this page.

Scale

Matrix questions used a 0–3 scale. The labelling differs per question:

  • Engineering tasks / Wagtail tasks: 0 (never) … 3 (always).
  • Possible improvements / CMS features: 0 (not helpful) … 3 (very helpful).
  • Adoption considerations: 0 (not important) … 3 (very important).

The original survey plan referenced a 0–4 scale; the deployed form was actually 0–3. All charts here reflect the deployed 0–3 scale.

Chart conventions

  • Each matrix question is shown three ways: (a) strongest signal — only the extreme ratings 0 and 3, x-axis clipped to ±50%; (b) full distribution clipped — all four ratings, x-axis at ±50% (some bars may extend past the visible area); (c) full distribution autoscaled — all four ratings without clipping. A fourth chart shows the average score per subtask.
  • Single-hue purple ramp from light to dark for the four ratings. This is intentionally a sequential rather than diverging palette — we did not want the visual encoding to suggest "no use is bad, heavy use is good".
  • Subtasks in each matrix and dot plot are ordered by average score, highest first.

Caveats

  • n = 48 is small and self-selected. Use the charts as a directional signal, not a statistical estimate.
  • Survey respondents skew towards Wagtail-engaged developers (the survey was distributed through Wagtail community channels), which likely biases adoption levels upwards relative to the broader Wagtail user base.
  • Free-text responses are not summarised on this page; see the corresponding CSVs in output/ for the raw answers.
